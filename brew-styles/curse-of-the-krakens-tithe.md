# The Curse in Brief

Decades ago, the legendary pirate vessel *The Kraken's Tithe* vanished from the high seas under mysterious circumstances. Now, its rotting hull has washed ashore on a bleak, fog-bound reef. Unaware of the nightmares waiting within, your party steps aboard to investigate. What begins as a search for answers quickly becomes a fight for survival against a vengeful past that refuses to stay buried.

This book is written for the GM. Boxed italic text is meant to be read aloud. Dossier boxes headed *Behind the Screen* hold secrets the players must earn. Ruled slabs hold history you may share as the party digs it out.

## What Is Really Going On

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**The curse.** The crew of *The Kraken's Tithe* bound the shore by magic when the ship was cursed. The mist is the trap: anyone who leaves on foot or by sea is returned to the same grey shingle. It does not kill. It holds. The lantern lights on the flat are the crew, searching for the way home.

**The binding.** The captain, the Tithe-Keeper, fed his crew salted bread mixed with black pearl dust, tying each sailor's blood to the ship. The cook refused the bread and hid a jar of salt under the galley stove. The black pearl, locked in the captain's chest, is the anchor of the curse.

**The pieces on the board.** Tobin Dorr is alive on the shore, and his name is in the ledger in fresh ink. Oriel Pask, a wrecker trapped for a season, has copied the tithe order and wants the pearl.
}}

## Corran Head

{{descriptive
The path over the headland drops into a steep valley of grey slate roofs, net-sheds and drying racks hung with split fish. Gulls wheel over the harbour wall, and the smell of brine and wood smoke is strong. The people here are quiet and watch strangers carefully, because the fog that hides the shore below has taken men before.
}}

{{tome
##### History
Corran Head was founded by fishers who wanted a harbour away from the rocks of the Greymist Shore. Calm summers brought their boats to the far beach, but since the Kraken's Tithe came out of the fog, no boat from Corran Head has landed there. The elders keep a bell on the harbour wall that is rung for every boat that does not return by dusk.
}}

#### Who Is Here
Maren Dorr mends nets by the harbour and asks strangers for help. The elder, Hobb Tarrant, keeps the harbour's one good boat and the records of who went out. The fishers are wary of anyone who talks about the Tithe.

#### What to Do Here
- Ask the elder about the missing boats. It costs a day's wait while he consults his tally.
- Buy a boat and crew for the voyage out. It costs most of the village's savings if the party cannot bargain.
- Rest and resupply in the net-sheds, where a cot and a hot meal are offered freely.

#### Nearby
The headland path leads down to the Greymist Shore, where the fog begins at the top of the shingle. Every boat that leaves the harbour is pulled back to that beach.

## Maren Dorr

Maren is thin and sunburnt, with salt-stiff hands and a grey wool shawl she never takes off. She speaks fast when frightened and slowly when she is angry.

Maren grew up in Corran Head with her brother Tobin, who was two years younger and the only one of the family who loved the sea. After their parents drowned, she mended nets and he fished. Three weeks ago his boat drifted back onto the shingle below the cliffs with its oars shipped and no one aboard. Maren has not slept properly since.

**Wants** :: Tobin back alive and the fog gone from her life.
**Fears** :: That the sea has taken him, and that she will spend her life waiting at the harbour wall.
**Sheet** :: AC 10, HP 10, speed 30 ft., all ability scores 8. Dagger, net, needles, rope, hooded lantern, spyglass, tinderbox, waterskin, 5 rations.

#### What She Knows
- Tobin's boat came back on the tide with its oars shipped, which means he was not thrown overboard.
- Tobin carried a brass compass with his initials scratched on the lid, and he never went out without it.
- The villagers say the fog on the shore has a voice, and that men who listen to it do not come back.

#### Meeting the Party
Maren finds the party in the harbour at dawn, holding out the lid of Tobin's compass, which she has kept as a memento. She offers forty gold from the village's collection and says she will row out herself if no one else will go.

#### Playing Her
Play Maren as practical and determined, not hysterical. She believes in the party's help only because she has no one else. She will argue with anyone who says Tobin is dead.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**What she hides.** Tobin is alive, but he is trapped on the Greymist Shore, where the mist returns him every time he tries to leave. He is pale and quiet and has begun to hear the crew's voices. Maren does not know that her brother's name has appeared in the Tithe's ledger.
}}

## Complications

- Tobin is near death from thirst and cold, and he has begun to hear the crew calling him toward the wreck. He may try to swim out to the Tithe at the first chance, and the party must stop him.
- Pask and his wreckers watch the flat from the dunes and try to take the party's lantern and rope before they reach the wreck.
- The tide turns in about an hour. The party must choose between searching the cabin with care and getting back to the shingle before the sea.
- Anyone who sits in the captain's chair hears the ledger read in the captain's voice and may find their own name written in the ledger.
- The crew on the decks stop and stare at anyone who touches the wheel or the bell. Ringing the bell draws them to the deck and may break the tide's hold on the flat.

## Rewards

Maren pays her forty gold, and Corran Head gives the party the *Lark's Due* and a crew to sail it, free of charge, for as long as the village has a boat. Freeing the crew and lifting the mist earns the thanks of the fishers and a share of the Tithe's old treasure in the hold. Keeping the pearl pays Pask's buyers, but the party gains a curse on the sea that follows them.

## If the Party Ignores It

Maren rows out herself a week later. The mist takes her as it took her brother, and her name appears in the ledger beneath his. Corran Head rings its bell every dusk for a boat that never comes back.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**Where Tobin is.** Tobin hides in the rock caves under the cliff path on the Greymist Shore. The rest of his compass dropped into a rock pool, where a careful search will find it. His name is in the Tithe's ledger.
}}

No one lives on the shore. Crabs, gulls and seals use the rocks, and nothing else is visible at first. The fog hides anything that moves beyond thirty paces.

## The Grey Shingle

{{descriptive
The party comes ashore out of a fog so thick that the cliffs show only as a darker wall above them. The air is cold and tastes of salt and rot, and the shingle grinds loudly under every step. Gulls cry somewhere unseen. When the tide is out, the sand flat stretches far to the west, and at its far edge a black hulk leans on its side, its ribs standing out of the sand like the bones of a whale.
}}

{{tome
##### History
The shore has never had a harbour or a village. Fishing boats from the coast beyond the headland sometimes landed here in calm summers, but the rocks and the sudden fogs made it a place sailors avoided. The Kraken's Tithe was driven onto the flat in a storm decades ago, or so the tellings say, and the sea has been slowly turning her into a reef since.
}}

#### What to Do Here
- **Walk the sand flat** at low tide to reach the wreck. The walk takes about an hour, and the tide turns quickly, so the party must return to the shingle before the water rises or be caught by the sea.
- **Search the rock pools and shingle** for anything the sea has left. Crabs and gulls are easy to find, but a careful search may also turn up a sailor's belt, a brass compass, or a ship's bell half buried in weed.
- **Climb the cliff path** to look for a way out of the fog. The path is slick and steep, and a fall costs real hit points. From the top the party can see the open sea, which only makes the looping mist more maddening.

#### Encounters
- Seals and gulls that flee when approached, though a wounded seal may attack.
- A wave that surges up the shingle while the party is on the flat, forcing a swift retreat or a dangerous scramble over the rocks.
- Ghostly lantern lights moving on the flat at dusk, which vanish when approached.

#### Nearby
The Kraken's Tithe lies on the sand flat to the west, reachable only at low tide. Every route away from the shore, by sea, cliff or inland path, leads back to this same shingle.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**What it hides.** The shore is the centre of the loop. However far the party walks, the mist returns them here. The lantern lights are the crew of the Tithe searching for the way home. The tide is the only true exit from the loop, since the flat remains open to the sea for a moment longer than the mist expects.
}}

## The Looping Mist

When the party tries to leave the shore, whether by walking inland, climbing the cliff path, or putting out in a boat, the fog thickens and they find themselves back on the shingle within the hour. The sea looks open from the cliff top, but a boat that pushes out is carried back to the same beach. The fog is cold and smells of salt and tar.

The mist is a magical trap set around the shore by the curse of *The Kraken's Tithe*. It does not kill the people it holds, but it returns them endlessly to the place where the cursed ship lies. Those who have been trapped for a long time grow pale and quiet, and some begin to hear the crew's voices.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**How it breaks.** The mist can be broken only by breaking the curse of the ship. The captain's chest holds the binding, and the black pearl feeds the loop. Breaking the pearl's hold lets the tide carry a boat out to open sea. Breaking the binding frees the crew, and the mist lifts for good.
}}

## Oriel Pask

{{quote
Nobody's leaving this beach, friend. We might as well make the best of it.
{{attribution Oriel Pask}}
}}

Pask is broad and sunburnt, with a grey beard braided with fishbone and a cutlass he keeps oiled. He speaks in a low, friendly voice and smiles when he is about to lie.

Pask was a salvager who worked the reefs down the coast, buying wrecks from fishers who did not ask questions. He came to the Greymist Shore a season ago looking for the Tithe's treasure. The mist caught him, and he has spent the months since searching the wreck, learning its ways and gathering a crew of wreckers who were trapped with him.

**Wants** :: The black pearl, and the buyers who will pay for it across the sea.
**Fears** :: The crew, who whisper to him at night, and being made to pay the tithe himself.

#### What He Knows
- The ledger lists the crew of the Tithe as "paid", and the tide decides when the mist lets go.
- The captain's chest must be opened with salt from the galley on the threshold, or the one who opens it is bound to the ship.
- A boat that is rowed hard against the tide reaches the sea only once in a while, and Pask has been waiting for that moment.

#### Meeting the Party
Pask meets the party on the sand flat, offering to trade a share of his findings for their boat, then turning hostile if they refuse. He tries to take any pearl or ledger the party finds before they reach the captain's cabin.

#### Playing Him
Play Pask as a charming rogue who believes everyone has a price. He drops the act the moment he feels cheated, and he keeps his word only when it suits him.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**What he hides.** Pask has copied the tithe order from the ledger in the captain's cabin, and he knows the names must be spoken in order to open the inner box. He plans to take the pearl once the crew are freed, because he believes the pearl will keep the mist, and his fortune, alive.

**His camp.** Anyone who searches Pask's camp in the dunes and finds a folded copy of the ledger's names, written in his own hand, learns that he means to take the pearl, not free the crew. He will deny it if asked directly.
}}

## The Wreck

{{descriptive
The galleon lies on her port side, her starboard hull torn open to the sky and her masts snapped to stumps. The smell of tar and rot is strong, and water still drips from the open timbers. Barnacles and kelp cover the lower planks, and a rope ladder hangs from the bulwark down into the sand. Above the wreck the fog moves slowly, as if it were breathing.
}}

{{tome
##### History
The Kraken's Tithe was a pirate galleon famous for the tithe she took from every ship she caught, a share of cargo and a share of crew who were never seen again. She vanished from the high seas decades ago. Her hull washed onto the shore in a storm, and in her hold the sea has preserved the remains of a crew that was bound by blood and magic to the ship. The curse ties the dead to the hull, and the mist keeps the living from leaving.
}}

#### What to Do Here
- **Climb the rope ladder** to the main deck and search the cabins, the galley and the forecastle for the ship's log, which records the tithe and the crew's names.
- **Descend into the flooded hold**, where cargo still lies in black water. Searching it is dangerous, but it may hold the curse's binding.
- **Ring the ship's bell**, which is said to call the crew to their stations. Doing so draws the dead to the deck and may break the tide's hold on the flat.
- **Pry open the captain's chest.** It contains the tithe's ledger and a black pearl that feeds the curse. Taking it binds the bearer to the ship.

#### Encounters
- Dead crew who wander the deck and the galley, and fight anyone who tries to take the captain's chest.
- A collapsed section of the hull that gives way underfoot, dropping a climber into the hold.
- Rising water in the hold that climbs with the tide, forcing a choice between searching quickly and leaving before the sea returns.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**What it hides.** The crew are not truly dead. They were bound to the ship when the Tithe was cursed, and the mist is their doing. Freeing them requires breaking the binding in the captain's chest, which means the party must choose between freeing the crew and sealing their fate. The black pearl is the anchor of the curse, and whoever holds it feeds the loop.
}}

## The Ship's Bell

A brass bell hangs from a cracked beam near the mainmast, green with age, with a rope still tied to its clapper. The deck around it is slick with weed, and the rope is easy to miss beneath it.

**Trap** :: Ringing the bell draws the drowned crew to the deck, where they stare at everyone nearby.
**Secret** :: The sound may break the tide's hold on the flat for a short while, giving the party a chance to reach the cabin. Each ring brings more of the dead, so the bell should be rung only when the party is ready to run.

## The Galley

{{descriptive
A narrow ladder drops from the aft hatch into a low room that smells of old smoke and rendered fat. A black iron stove stands against the far wall, its firebox full of ash that is strangely dry. Copper pots hang from hooks and sway slightly, though the rest of the ship is still. A long table runs down the middle, laid with pewter bowls and spoons, each holding a grey, skinned stew.
}}

{{tome
##### History
The Tithe's cook fed the crew twice a day and kept the galley locked when the captain was not in it. The cook kept a tally of every meal on the wall beside the stove, one notch per mouth. Near the end, the notches stop being counted by mouths and start being counted by bowls set aside for "the sea's share".
}}

No cook is here, only the table, the stove and the bowls. A chill sits in the room, and the air is thick enough that a voice from the hatch sounds muffled.

#### What to Do Here
- **Study the notches** beside the stove. Counting them against the bowls reveals that there is always one more bowl than there were crew.
- **Search the stove** and the flour bin for a hidden jar of coarse salt.
- **Taste the stew.** Those who eat it hear the crew's voices calling their names in the hold for an hour, which gives a useful clue but costs them sleep and a little strength.

#### Encounters
- The bowls refill with warm stew whenever someone sits at the table, and the spoons scrape by themselves.
- A pot lid lifts and clatters down when the party turns its back, and the stove door creaks open a finger's width.
- A dead cook's shape stands at the stove, turning a ladle, and fades when looked at directly.

### The Galley Stove
An iron stove against the bulkhead, its firebox full of grey ash. Something is buried in the ash beneath the grate.

**Concealment** :: The jar is buried under the ash and grate. A careful search reveals it, and a rushed one may miss it.
**Contents** :: A sealed jar of coarse salt, bound with wax and twine.
**Secret** :: Searching carelessly, with a fire-poker or a bare hand, may spill the ash and bring the dead cook's attention to the galley.

### The Cook's Tally
A row of notches scratched into the galley bulkhead, each one beside a tin bowl hung on a hook. Some bowls are worn bright from use, and others are dull with rust.

**Concealment** :: Visible to anyone in the galley. The meaning of the marks shows only to a careful count.
**Secret** :: The cook marked the bowls that were never eaten from, the bowls of the captain's bread. A careful reader learns that the cook refused it, which points the party to the salt hidden in the stove.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**What the galley hides.** The cook refused to bind the crew. The captain fed the crew salted bread with black pearl dust in it, which tied each sailor's blood to the ship, and the cook hid a jar of salt under the stove. The salt, sprinkled on the threshold of the captain's cabin, breaks the first binding and lets one sailor's name be spoken free. The extra bowl is the captain's, and it is never eaten.
}}

## The Crew Berths

{{descriptive
The forward ladder leads down into a crowded space low enough that a tall adult must stoop. Rows of hammocks hang from the beams, swaying gently as if a sea were running. Above each hammock a name is cut into the wood, and the carvings are neat, as if a careful hand made them. The smell is of damp wool, tar and a sweetish rot.
}}

{{tome
##### History
The crew slept here in shifts, and the captain sent each new sailor to the berths with a bowl of bread after the first night at sea. Those who slept well were marked as the ship's own. Those who woke screaming were sent to the hold. The carvings were made by the sailors themselves, each cutting his own name, and some of them cut more deeply than others.
}}

No living crew remain. In some hammocks a sleeper's outline lies under the blanket, and a hand sometimes shows at the edge of one, still holding a belt or a knife.

#### What to Do Here
- **Read the names** carved over the hammocks and compare them to the captain's ledger. Some names are cut deeper, which marks the sailors who were bound by the bread.
- **Search the sea chests** at the end of the room. One holds a sailor's journal that describes the night the bread was first given out.
- **Sleep in a hammock** to see the crew's dreams. The dreams show the galley and the cargo hold, but they cost the sleeper a clear memory of the way back to the shingle.
- **Cut down a hammock.** The sailor's body is wrapped in a shroud with a bronze token pressed into its mouth.

#### Encounters
- Hammocks sway harder when someone approaches a deep-cut name, and a voice counts down from ten.
- A sleeper sits up slowly and asks, in a tired voice, whether it is time for the watch.
- The lamps on the beams flicker, and the shadows of the crew line up along the walls facing the ladder.

### The Bunk Names
Names cut into the bunk boards, one per bunk. The dead lie in some of them, and their eyes follow anyone who reads the names.

**Concealment** :: Carved names are worn. A bunk with a fresh name stands out to a close reader.
**Secret** :: Reading the names aloud calms the dead, and reading them in tithe order from the ledger calms them most. Reading a name that is not on the crew list lets the dead rise and attack. One bunk bears the name **Tobin Dorr**, cut fresh and sharp.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**What the berths hide.** The deep-cut names are the sailors who were bound first, and their hammocks are the anchors of their curse. Cutting one down without first speaking the name aloud gives the bound sailor's ghost a chance to pull a living sleeper into the dream, where they remain until someone else sleeps in that hammock.
}}

## The Cargo Hold

{{descriptive
A hatch in the forward deck opens onto a ladder that drops into cold, black water. The hold stretches far into the dark, lined with crates, barrels and rope. Pale shapes hang from the beams, wrapped in shrouds that sway when the water stirs. The air is wet and metallic, and every drip echoes long after it falls.
}}

{{tome
##### History
The Tithe did not sail with trade cargo alone. The captain's share of every seized ship was stored here, and the crews of those ships were lashed in the hold until the tithe was counted. Those who were not bound by bread were bound by rope, and the sea rose to take them as the ship sank into her own curse.
}}

No living people remain here. The shrouded figures on the beams are the taken crews, and a drowned sailor sometimes rises from the bilge and sinks again.

#### What to Do Here
- **Search the barrels** for salt, which marks the cargo of the captain's share. Some barrels are full of teeth and bone instead.
- **Cut a shroud down.** Its occupant is a drowned sailor whose name is written on the cloth, and the shroud falls into the water and must be recovered quickly.
- **Work the winch** by the hatch to raise the cargo nets. The winch chain is slick with weed, and it pulls the hatch closed if turned too far.
- **Follow the bilge line** along the hull, where a manifest of names is scratched into the timber under the waterline.

#### Encounters
- A drowned sailor rises from the bilge, reaching out with bloated fingers, then sinks again.
- Shrouds turn to face the party when the water rises, and whisper the names on their cloth.
- The hatch slams shut and the water climbs a foot in a minute, forcing a swift exit or a grim wade through the cargo.

### The Cargo Crates
Crates wedged between the ribs, rising above the black water. Their stencils name merchant houses and ports, and a sodden flag hangs from one of them.

**Lock** :: Stiff, swollen lids. A Strength check forces one open.
**Trap** :: Prying a crate open without care wakes the drowned crew in the hold, who rise from the water to reclaim their share.
**Contents** :: Coins in a sealed purse, trade goods in waxed cloth, and a sodden flag. This is the Tithe's old treasure, which Corran Head would reward the party with if the crew is freed.

{{note
##### Behind the Screen
**What the hold hides.** The shrouded figures are the taken crews, not the Tithe's own men, and they are not fully dead. Their names on the cloth match the entries in the ledger. A party that frees one of them without salt on its wrist sees its ghost drown again. Salt scattered on the water turns the bilge back into an ordinary bilge, and the drowned sailor's name is then free to be spoken.
}}

## The Captain's Cabin

{{descriptive
A heavy oak door at the top of the aft companionway stands ajar, its brass handle green with verdigris. Inside, the cabin is panelled in dark wood that has warped and split. Sunlight from the broken stern windows falls on a carved desk, a high-backed chair and a portrait of a broad-shouldered man in a red coat. Someone has scratched out his eyes with a knife. An iron-bound chest sits under the stern window.
}}

{{tome
##### History
The captain of the Tithe was called the Tithe-Keeper by his crew, who took a tenth of every cargo and a tenth of every crew he sailed with. He kept his tithe ledger here, recording each ship's name and each man taken aboard. He believed the sea would be satisfied by blood, and the curse was his way of keeping the ship from ever sinking.
}}

#### Encounters
The ledger's ink runs and reforms into a new name, usually of someone who has touched the desk. The portrait's painted eyes are wet, and the wet trail reaches the frame's edge. The door slams shut and the chest's lid swings open, with a chime like a ship's bell.

### The Captain's Ledger
A thick, water-stained ledger lies open on the chart table. Its names are listed in tithe order, and its last page holds a fresh name in wet ink.

**Concealment** :: The fresh name is hard to notice at a glance and shows clearly only on a close read.
**Contents** :: The crew's names in tithe order, and at the end, **Tobin Dorr**, in ink that runs and reforms whenever someone touches the desk. It can be read only in the cabin.
**Secret** :: Sitting in the captain's chair and reading the ledger aloud lets the captain's voice read it back, and the reader may find their own name written beneath the others.

### The Black Pearl
Inside the captain's chest lies a dark pearl the size of a plover's egg, with a grey-black sheen that shifts when looked at directly. It is wrapped in oiled silk, and it is the anchor of the curse. Whoever holds it is bound to the ship.

### The Pinned Captain
The captain's skeleton hangs from the panelled wall, pinned by his coat and by a saber driven into the timber. His bony fingers are curled around a crumpled sheet of paper.

**Trap** :: A rough tug on the paper snaps the fingers. The skeleton falls and rises to guard the chest.
**Creature** :: The Tithe-Keeper's remains, bound to the cabin (see Bestiary).
**Secret** :: The paper is the Captain's Paper (see Handouts). Pulling the saber before reading it turns the captain's skull toward the thief.

### The Wedged Saber
A long curved saber with a serpent-shaped brass guard, driven deep into the panelling. The wood around the blade is dark and wet.

**Lock** :: A Strength check pulls the blade free. Failure leaves it wedged and bleeding.
**Contents** :: A saber, usable as a weapon once freed. Whoever wields it is marked by the captain's mark, and the dead crew will recognise it.

### The Captain's Chest
An iron-bound chest beneath the stern window. Its lid is locked, and its brass corners are green with verdigris.

**Lock** :: Locked. It opens only after the salt is spread on the threshold and the ledger names are spoken in tithe order.
**Trap** :: Opening it without the salt on the threshold cuts the opener's palm and binds them to the ship as the captain's heir.
**Concealment** :: The lock plate is visible from the door. The salt crust shows on close inspection.
**Contents** :: A locked inner box, the black pearl wrapped in oiled silk, and the binding seal.

### The Captain's Mirror
A tall cheval mirror stands in the corner, its silvered glass clouded with age. Its reflection lags a moment behind the room.

**Trap** :: When a player stares into the glass too long, or touches it, the reflection steps out and attacks as a doppelganger built from the victim's own sheet at half their maximum hit points. It cannot leave the room, and it slides back into the glass when destroyed.
