{{chapter
# The Hanging Bell
}}

The bell had not rung in forty years, yet every soul in Karrowmere swore they heard it the night the river ran black. Fathers barred their doors. Mothers pressed cold coins to the eyes of the sleeping. In the tower, something *breathed*.

No priest will speak of what the old bell-ringer found beneath the belfry. The dead there do not lie quietly, and the living who go looking are seldom seen again. Those who return carry a **debt** they cannot name and a *silence* they cannot break.

The river keeps its own counsel. It carries the drowned past the mill without a ripple, and the miller, who was once a gentle man, now sets out salt at his threshold and will not meet the eyes of strangers. The reeve counts the missing at each new moon and has run out of ledger.

### The Cinder Road

Three days north of the last honest hearth, the road becomes ash. Nothing grows. The wind carries the taste of iron, and the crows will not follow.

- Travel through the Cinder Road without rest costs each traveller a level of exhaustion.
- Fires lit after dusk draw **hollow things** within the hour.
- Anyone who speaks the name *Karrow* aloud must succeed on a DC 13 Wisdom save or hear the bell.

{{descriptive
The door swings inward before you touch it. Beyond, a hall of black stone, and at its end a figure kneels, hands bound with wire, its head bowed as though in prayer. The air stinks of tallow and old blood. *It is humming a lullaby.*
}}

Beyond the road lies the village itself: forty roofs, one well, and a gibbet that has never once stood empty. The villagers do not look up when the bell is mentioned. They look *down*, at their own hands, as though counting.

### What the Ledger Says

The Margrave's reeve keeps two books. The first lists the living. The second, bound in something that is not quite leather, lists everyone else. Both are kept scrupulously up to date, and the second is always longer.

### The Lords of Ruin

Karrowmere is held by three powers, none of them kind. Each demands tribute. Each keeps the others from winning outright, and in this uneasy balance the common folk are ground fine.

{{tome
##### Of the Pale Margrave
The Margrave rules from a keep of bone-white stone. He has not slept in thirty winters, and his court has stopped pretending not to notice.

He pays his soldiers in salt and silence.
}}

# The Reliquary

The vault beneath the tower is cold in a way that has nothing to do with temperature. Shelves of fused bone climb into the dark, each hung with a small iron tag. The tags bear names. Some bear *yours*.

### What the Dead Keep

Every relic here holds a **memory** the dead refuse to surrender. To take one is to inherit it. To destroy one is to wake whatever keeps it.

- A rib that remembers a drowning.
- A tooth that remembers a promise.
- A finger-bone that points, always, toward the one who broke it.

{{descriptive
Your torchlight finds a shape on the floor, a shape that was a man and is now a stain. Beside it, a single line scratched into the stone: **"I TOLD THEM IT WAS HUNGRY."**
}}

{{note
